Act I • Aurora & Traditio

The Morning & Chinese Tea Ceremony

Before the bells of St. Peter’s echoed across the Tiber, our wedding day dawned within the historic chambers of Residenza Napoleone III in Palazzo Ruspoli. Surrounded by antique Renaissance boiserie and Roman morning light, we began our union with the traditional Chinese Tea Ceremony (敬茶), honoring our parents, ancestors, and centuries of family heritage.

Tradition • 敬茶 Filial Piety • 孝

The Chinese Tea Ceremony

Honoring Parents • Ancestral Blessings

Kneeling before our parents, we served sweet tea infused with red dates and lotus seeds—symbolizing sweetness, purity, and abundant blessings in marriage.

  • • The Tea Serving (敬茶): An ancient rite of gratitude and devotion, offering tea with two hands to parents as a gesture of deep reverence and respect.
  • • Red Envelopes (红包 & 黄金): Parents bestowed heirloom gold jewelry and auspicious red packets (hóngbāo), welcoming the bride and groom into their shared lineage.
  • • Two Families Become One: Uniting Chinese traditions with Italian warmth, sharing moments of intimate tears, joy, and ancestral blessings.
Auspicious Red • 囍 Palazzo Ruspoli
Palazzo Ruspoli • 1556 Imperial Residence

Residenza Napoleone III

Via Condotti • Rome

Built in the 16th century on the corner of Via Condotti and Corso, the residence hosted Prince Louis Napoleon (later Emperor Napoleon III) in 1830.

  • • Gilded Boiserie & Frescoes: The imperial suite features 18th-century antique boiserie, monumental oil paintings, and hand-carved canopies designed for French royalty.
  • • The Monumental Staircase: 100 solid marble steps, once regarded as one of the “Four Wonders of Rome,” creating a regal setting for morning bridal portraits.
  • • Roman Morning Light: Soft amber rays pouring through tall palace windows as silk, qipao, and veil were arranged in peaceful anticipation.
Via del Corso • Roma Morning Portraits
The Journey • 10:30 Tiber Crossing

Crossing the Tiber

Palazzo Ruspoli → Piazza San Pietro

With tea ceremony blessings concluded, the wedding party stepped into the Roman morning, driving past the Spanish Steps, Piazza Navona, and crossing the river toward the Vatican.

  • • Bridal Radiance: Sara in her bespoke white bridal gown, framed against the ancient cobblestones of Rome.
  • • Passage to St. Peter’s: Passing beneath Bernini’s colossal marble colonnade as the Vatican bells chimed noon for the holy liturgy.
  • • From Palace to Basilica: Linking the private intimacy of home and family heritage with the timeless, universal sacredness of the Catholic Sacrament.

Act II • Fides & Sacramentum

Sacred Vows at St. Peter’s Basilica

Bringing together Sara’s Chinese heritage and Tiziano’s Italian roots, we chose as a Catholic couple to exchange our vows at the spiritual heart of the universal Church—welcoming our families and friends to experience the faith, timeless art, and architectural grandeur of Vatican City and Rome.

I • Sacred Liturgy CONSECRATED 1626

Cappella del Coro

St. Peter’s Basilica • Vatican City

Designed by Carlo Maderno on the south nave of the Basilica for the solemn chanting of the Divine Office, the Chapel of the Choir is one of the Vatican’s most intimate baroque sanctuaries.

  • • Bernini’s Gilded Vault: High-relief golden stuccoes designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini and executed by G.B. Ricci depicting Sacred Scripture and the glory of creation.
  • • Altar of the Immaculate Conception: An 18th-century mosaic after Pietro Bianchi framed by Egyptian alabaster and porphyry, crowned with a golden tiara by Pope Pius IX in 1854.
  • • 17th-Century Choir Stalls: Deeply carved walnut stalls and twin pipe organs where centuries of papal polyphony have echoed.
Sacrament of Matrimony Vatican Nave
II • The Procession 134 AD – 1667

Colonnade & Tiber Passage

Piazza San Pietro → Ponte Sant’Angelo

Emerging from the Basilica into the sunlight of Rome, our path traced the ancient pilgrimage route connecting the Tomb of the Apostle to the historic hills of the Eternal City.

  • • Bernini’s Colonnade (1656–1667): 284 colossal travertine columns surmounted by 140 saints, conceived by Bernini as the “maternal arms of the Church” embracing guests from every nation.
  • • Ponte Sant’Angelo (134 AD): Erected by Emperor Hadrian as the Pons Aelius over the Tiber River and transformed in 1669 by Bernini’s ten baroque marble Angels of the Passion.
  • • East Meets West: A symbolic crossing linking two millennia of Roman history as family from China, Italy, and beyond processed toward the Pincian Hill.
Pilgrims’ Way Flumen Tiberis
III • The Convivium BUILT 1816–1837

Casina Valadier

Pincian Hill (Il Pincio) • Villa Borghese

Perched on the highest belvedere of the Pincian Hill, this jewel of Roman Neoclassicism was fashioned by master architect Giuseppe Valadier, the visionary planner of Piazza del Popolo below.

  • • Ancient Roman Foundations: Built directly atop the vaulted Roman cisterns of the 1st-century BC Horti Aciliorum, the hillside estate of the patrician Acilii Glabriones.
  • • Salone degli Specchi: Adorned with delicate Pompeian-revival frescoes, ionic loggias, and neoclassical symmetry celebrating classical antiquity.
  • • The Vatican Vista: Its west-facing terraces look straight across Rome’s domes back to Michelangelo’s cupola of St. Peter’s, uniting morning vows with evening celebration.
Neoclassical Belvedere Collis Pincius
Polyphonia Sacra • Cappella Giulia

During our marriage service, his motet Sicut Cervus (Psalm 42) resonated through Maderno’s walnut stalls. Act III • Lux & Convivium

The Reception at Casina Valadier

Ascending from the Vatican through the heart of Rome, our celebration gathered atop the Pincian Hill at Casina Valadier. Designed by neoclassical architect Giuseppe Valadier inside the lush gardens of Villa Borghese, the villa offered an open terrace overlooking Rome as the golden sun set behind St. Peter’s dome.

Neoclassical Villa • 1817 Il Pincio

Casina Valadier

Pincian Hill • Villa Borghese

Restructured between 1816 and 1837 by Giuseppe Valadier over a former 17th-century casino, the building embodies pure neoclassical proportion with Ionic columns and panoramic loggias.

  • • The Highest Point of Rome: Gazing west toward Piazza del Popolo, the Tiber, and the silhouette of St. Peter’s.
  • • Garden Terraces: Umbrella pines and historic gravel paths where guests sipped prosecco and Roman aperitivi.
Villa Borghese Gardens Roman Belvedere

3 Violins & Basso Continuo
